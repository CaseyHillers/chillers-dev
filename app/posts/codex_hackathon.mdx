Recently I got to participate in a [Codex](https://platform.openai.com/docs/Codex) hackathon hosted by OpenAI for my organization.

For context, I am a Codex power user. My workday has me using it for at least half the day, and I find that I am putting it through its paces on random personal projects. Recently, the Codex UI app has me spinning up multiple sessions to work on things in parallel. I'm still experimenting with automations to help with my tedious project management work.

One reflection I had, assuming our work is representative of the industry, is that if you're using Codex you are an early adopter. The reason this hackathon was put on was to encourage people to use it, and less so to show off what could be done with it.

On the flip side, I was humbled by my lack of imagination. Several people trying Codex for the first time used it to generate their demo videos! Codex was able to generate images, stitch them into a video, and add a voice over. They weren't high quality, but it's a great way to validate a script before investing time on it.

One of the cool things I learned from the OpenAI engineers was about [Codex's skill creator](https://developers.openai.com/Codex/skills/). I have been lazy around creating these as I thought MCP was still preferred. However, the demos they showed were fast and effective. I applied it to my own codebase and built three superpowered abilities in 30 minutes.

For the hackathon, we were given 4 hours and teams of 4 to get something to show off using Codex. Naively, our team wanted to work together on it, but those plans quickly fell apart. It became apparent that none of us knew how to work on small projects with others and Codex at the same time. The closest we got was trying out each other's demos.

Overall, it was a good experience, and taught me to do more with Codex.

PS: Anecdotally, I have come to the conclusion that hiring someone without experience using Codex/Gemini CLI/Claude Code is like hiring an Amish person today. I bring this up as I was trying to encourage a family member to start using these tools at work. The defense contractor they work at had spent a year building out Anthropic to run on a GovCloud instance.
