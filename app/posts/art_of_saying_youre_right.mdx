One of my favorite LLM jokes on the internet is:

> Think of the dumbest person you know. They have Claude telling them they are absolutely right.

While there has been significant work to reduce AI sycophancy, we still face a fundamental hurdle: human nature. At the end of the day, people want to be told only the good news: they are right, they are making good progress, they look good in their outfit.

I have heard versions of this story from so many people. They truly believed an idea because it seemed like someone else was bought into it. For example:

- **The failed launch:** Starting a business and never getting a single customer because they thought the LLM was enough to determine if there was a market for it
- **The ruined breakfast:** Adding ginger to their breakfast yogurt bowl cause its their favorite ingredient and it has health benefits
- **Hitting the wall:** Being told your training plan is enough for the race, and half way through that race realizing you came in 20% short

I don't think this is an issue LLMs will be able to solve. Before algorithms, we had yes-men.

<PostImage src="https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/toontownrewritten/images/c/c0/Cog-bossbot-yesman.png" alt="Yesman robot from the video game Toontown" />

It is deeply wired into the human psyche to love being *glazed,* and we trap ourselves with those that continue to do it. LLMs are chat bots that are heavily optimized for engagement. They are gamified to keep you coming back to them.

## What can I do?

- **[Social capital](https://newsletter.rafapaez.com/p/social-capital-the-career-edge-most):** Rarely can you actually tell people they are wrong. You need to be having open conversations. Praise the good, and let them know when things need correcting.
- **Adversial Reviews:** If you are relying on LLMs for your work, screen their work by other LLMs. I find myself having Codex generated artifacts be reviewed by Claude Opus as a great force multiplier. There's still things I catch afterwards, but it catches a mulititude of things before I spend time on it. An example I like to share is having Codex review itself is like letting students grade their own work...
- **Normalize the uncomfortable:** Most of our relationships are going to be superficial. We want people to like us. Find those critical people in your life, and set the ground rule to be honest with each other. This isn't your roast partner, so make sure you thank them and give praise for the difficult conversations.

## Authenticity in AI

LLMs are built on almost all human knowledge. It is amazing how we can make the most sophisticated digital brain fit on computers today, and use it to reason about vast sets of tasks. One could argue that when you pair these models with advanced coding harnesses, we are already looking at a form of AGI.

We can try to grow by leveraging AI, but the human mind is great at pattern recognition. It knows when even the smallest of texts was written by an LLM, which is why I pain stakingly take the time to write these blog posts. My brain, nor any ones brain, will ever be able to surpass the knowledge of an LLM.

Neither my brain nor yours will ever surpass the raw knowledge database of an LLM. But what we can do is find the ideas that are off the beaten path.

True growth doesn't come from a mirror - it comes from looking out the window.
