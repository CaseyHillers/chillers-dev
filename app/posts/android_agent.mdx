As the lead on the Android agent project, I worked across our cloud and Android stacks to create the first Android agent. Using state-of-the-art multimodal models from frontier labs, we combined vision with the model's ability to output commands for driving the device and formulating plans to achieve any requested task.

<YouTube id="_Kg8G41ZvHU" title="Research Preview: Android Agent" />

### Beyond Simple Device Driving

Agents driving computers wasn't necessarily what was novel, but what was interesting was our use of **Android's Virtual Display**. By leveraging virtual displays, we unlocked two critical capabilities:

1. **Background Execution**: We could run tasks in the background without blocking the user's active screen or interrupting their flow.
2. **Dynamic Screen Sizing**: We could size the virtual display dynamically to the ideal aspect ratio for a given app or task. For faster, simpler tasks, we simulated smartphones (smaller screen size, meaning fewer tokens per screenshot/step). For information-intense tasks like web research, we spun up a tablet-sized interface.

While doing everything on an Android agent wasn't always optimal, having this capability run on a cheap, physical Android device was the cool part. It empowered people outside of massive data centers to create and deploy their own local agents.

### Industry Impact

One of the most rewarding parts of the project was seeing the immense interest from major smartphone manufacturers, which led to opportunities to demo the agent directly to their VPs. 

It's also been fascinating to watch the space evolve since then. Google has recently gotten close to implementing something very similar in Android and Gemini with their [Gemini multi-step task functionality](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/android-multi-step-tasks/).
