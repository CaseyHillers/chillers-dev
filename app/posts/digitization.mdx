The past few years I have become a local expert in digitizing physical media. I have spent a few too many weekends being the go-to-guy to help clean out family houses and get our sentimental media online.

## 2021

2021 was the first house I worked on. A family member nearby had grown recluse during covid, and became a hoarder. The difficult part about this house was that there was so much stuff. I wanted to make sure we didn't throw out or sell anything important. To show the magnitude, we did a garage sale for 3 days. Every day we would make two trips to bring stuff to sell, resulting in the side of the house filled with the shipping boxes.

<PostImage src="/digitizing_garage_boxes.jpg" alt="Large amount of collapsed boxes on the side of the garage" caption="Collapsed boxes after the three-day garage sale." style={{ height: '500px', width: 'auto' }} />

After several weeks with my family working on clearing out this house, we ended up with the final boxes. These were photographs, letters, and negatives.

I wanted to start with negatives we found. We had gotten a large box of negatives with just over 100 shots. The interesting thing here is you can't easily filter out which were the good negatives. So it's a tedious process as you'll scan everything. I was able to buy a license for VueScan and borrow a scanner to go through these negatives. It was pretty fast as you could load up 5 at a time, then VueScan applied some clean up and cropping for the images. I don't think a lot of these negatives had been looked at in 60 years, so it was a bummer to see that some of them were blurry.

## 2025

Several years later, and I'm back to clean out my parents house. There was much less stuff to get rid of, but I did want to digitize as much as possible.

The interesting thing about this effort is it felt more like an engineering project. I had all these assets, and I wanted to minimize the time I spent processing them. I leveraged my Google AI Pro subscription that came free with my phone to use the Gemini CLI and AI Studio to quickly build out the necessary tooling.

### VHS Tapes

The new media type for me was VHS tapes. Growing up, we had VHS tapes laying around, but I can't remember ever using them. I spent a while trying to find a VHS player. A lot of used electronics stores near me were selling them for over $100! For 20 year old technology that likely doesn't work, that's crazy.

I had about 60 VHS tapes to go through. Companies offer digitization services, but charge $20 a tape. That totals over $1200 for this project. Additionally, you need to pay a premium to get back your tapes, and if the company destroys in the process you are out of luck.

One weekend a friend got lucky and found me a VHS player on one of the community feeds. I got it home, and was able to verify it worked!

With that, I was in business. My concern for digitization was around how to get the output from the player onto my computer. The first thing on a Google search is a $120 hardware dongle that connects to an AV player and writes the recordings to a USB dongle. I had mulled over this project for a while, and thought about getting an AV -> HDMI cable, and then getting a HDMI -> USB C capture card. This ended up costing me $35, and was a very light weight way of digitizing the tapes.

The painful part about recording the tapes is that you have to play it back at 1:1 speed. So if a tape has 3 hours of content, its going to take 3 hours to playback.

Out of 60 tapes, 4 were unable to be played. I was able to 3D print a manual VHS rewinder. The belief was they had glued themselves shut, and some extra TLC would get them moving again. I would spend a few minutes twisting the gears on the tapes to get them going again.

After 5 days, I was able to digitize all the boxes of tapes. I ended up with 15 hours of tapes to go through. Each tape could have several smaller tapes on it. Think family Christmas, a TV movie, then a house tour. This was a tedious process to find and cut the precise points.

I decided to have Google's AI Studio build me a web app that could process the uploaded video recordings, use AI to find the cut points, and create a movie editor to create the different clips.

In one shot, it got 99% of what I want. I followed up with 4 more tweaks, mostly around usability. The longer videos were 3 hours, so that resulted in Gemini rate limit errors. Another was you could only cut clips by a tenth of a second, when realistically I was doing around 5 seconds at a time.

After 10 minutes, I had the perfect solution. Little did I know, it came up with a clever solution for processing these videos. It would cut frames every 10 seconds, then classify the content. Then it would do a second pass to group all the summaries together to get the different content. This wasn't a perfect solution, but it worked 95% of the way. I would've loved the ability to have it actually process the video. For example, I really like to see my direct family, so calling out their names to the AI to highlight would have been a nice feature.

Web apps are limited, so my vibe coded app generated JSON files for me to download. I then passed over the project context to the Gemini CLI, and had it use ffmpeg with the JSON to cut into the relevant clips.

### Scanning Photos

I remembered photos being a time consuming aspect. I was using the same scanner, and it took about 10 seconds to capture the photo.

One optimization I thought about was putting multiple photos on the scanner at once, and post process them later with an edge detection algorithm.

In theory, I could do 4 photos at a time which was a great improvement! In practice, it ended up being ~2.5x since some photos were bigger than the standard 4x6.

I hand wrote a python script to do edge detection on my scanned JPGs. In simple cases, this worked well. I had issues when it came to having multiple photos on a raw JPG where the remaining photos didn't easily get picked up. I spent a while working on this, implementing more advanced algorithms, and had no luck.

I thought about it overnight, and magically Meta released [SAM3](https://ai.meta.com/research/sam3/) the next day. SAM3 is an image classification model, and is very overkill for what I needed. It can give masks for where the rabbits are in a photo. I loaded it up onto my 3060, and asked it to give me "photographs" and processed the raw scans. It was super successful! It gives non-rectangular polygons, but that was a simple fix by getting the bounds for each of the 4 points in a rectangle.

## Takeaways

- The hardest part about digitizing is getting the hardware that supports your efforts
- Store media even if it's blurry, poor lighting, or damaged. There's a good chance one day we'll have the technology that can recover it.
- Find peace in the tedium required to digitize media
